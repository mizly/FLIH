SIMULATOR CHANGES NEEDED TO MATCH THE PHYSICAL ROBOT
=====================================================

Scope
-----
This is only the physical sim-to-hardware alignment list. It does not cover model
loading, PyTorch installation, the live inference loop, goal management, logging,
or motor safety software.


1. CAMERAS
----------

1.1 Replace the nominal camera intrinsics with measurements from both physical
    camera modules.

    Measure independently for sensor 1 (left) and sensor 0 (right):
    - Horizontal and vertical focal length.
    - Principal point.
    - Radial and tangential lens distortion.
    - Usable horizontal and vertical field of view.

    Current simulator assumption:
    - Ideal pinhole camera.
    - 69.47 degrees horizontal by 42.61 degrees vertical.
    - No distortion.
    - Principal point exactly at image centre.

    Current nominal physical-lens estimate:
    - Approximately 62.19 degrees horizontal by 48.74 degrees vertical.
    - Only about 87 percent of the simulator's horizontal view is physically
      covered. The current rectification therefore creates black/missing regions
      of approximately 3.64 degrees on each horizontal side.

    After calibration, either change the simulated camera FOV to the measured FOV
    and retrain, or retain the existing simulated FOV and simulate the same black
    rectification borders seen on the robot.

1.2 Replace the drawing-based camera mounts with measured chassis extrinsics.

    Measure for each camera:
    - Position relative to the robot centre: x, y, and z.
    - Outward yaw.
    - Downward pitch.
    - Roll.
    - Left-to-right baseline and optical-axis convergence.

    Current simulator assumption:
    - Left centre:  (0.103,  0.035, 0.175) m.
    - Right centre: (0.103, -0.035, 0.175) m.
    - 29.73 degrees outward yaw per camera.
    - 5 degrees downward pitch.
    - Zero roll.

    Stereo calibration alone is insufficient because it does not locate the camera
    pair relative to the chassis or ground.

1.3 Match the physical camera acquisition timing.

    Current simulator/training assumption:
    - A new camera image every 20 ms (50 Hz policy/control step).

    Physical camera mode:
    - 1640x1232 full-array capture at 30 Hz.

    The simulator should eventually reproduce 30 Hz camera updates held between
    50 Hz policy steps, including real capture latency and jitter. This matters to
    the learned L1/L2/L3 temporal filters.

1.4 Match the physical image pipeline and camera imperfections.

    Add measured or representative:
    - Exposure and automatic-exposure changes.
    - White-balance and colour-response differences between the two modules.
    - Sensor noise and compression-free capture characteristics.
    - Motion blur.
    - Rolling-shutter effects.
    - Focus variation and lens vignetting.
    - Dropped, delayed, repeated, and unsynchronised left/right frames.

    The simulated images are RGB. Physical OpenCV frames are BGR; the live adapter
    must convert them, while simulator training data should reproduce the resulting
    RGB appearance.

1.5 Preserve the confirmed physical eye assignment in every simulation/deployment
    comparison:
    - CSI sensor 1 is the left camera.
    - CSI sensor 0 is the right camera.


2. LIDAR
--------

2.1 Replace the nominal LiDAR pose with a measured pose.

    Measure:
    - x, y, and scan-plane height relative to the robot centre.
    - Mount yaw relative to the robot's true driving-forward direction.
    - Roll and pitch/optical tilt.

    Current simulator assumption:
    - Centre: (0.0, 0.0, 0.210) m.
    - Perfectly level scan plane.
    - Zero angle is robot-forward.

    The physical driver currently uses a measured device-zero offset of 270 degrees.
    Recheck this after any scanner movement and ensure the simulator and physical
    robot use the same definition of forward.

2.2 Match the measured scan rate and point density.

    Current simulator assumption:
    - 6 Hz.
    - 4000 samples/second.
    - 667 uniformly spaced rays per revolution.

    Physical scanner:
    - Operates in the 6-12 Hz range.
    - The vendor configuration may run at 10 Hz, producing roughly 400 points per
      revolution instead of 667.
    - Samples are not perfectly uniformly spaced.

    Measure the actual steady scan rate. Configure the scanner to 6 Hz or change the
    simulator to the measured rate and retrain.

2.3 Simulate a rolling revolution instead of an instantaneous scan.

    A 6 Hz physical revolution spans about 167 ms. At 0.4 m/s the robot can move
    roughly 6.7 cm during one scan. Simulate per-ray acquisition times, robot motion
    during the turn, completion latency, and holding the last complete scan between
    updates.

2.4 Replace ideal ranges with measured sensor behavior.

    Add or calibrate:
    - Range bias and random noise versus distance.
    - Minimum and effective maximum range.
    - Lower effective range on dark/low-reflectivity material.
    - Missing returns.
    - Sunlight interference.
    - Glass/reflective-surface failures.
    - Thin-object misses.
    - Packet loss and incomplete revolutions.

    Current simulation treats missing/out-of-range returns as 12 m and does not model
    reflectivity, flags, or uncertainty.

2.5 Model the real 2D scan plane and robot self-occlusion.

    Confirm which chassis parts, camera mounts, cables, or deck edges intersect the
    physical scan plane. The simulator currently excludes all robot geometry from
    LiDAR rays. Include any consistent physical self-returns rather than filtering
    them only on hardware.


3. CHASSIS AND COLLISION GEOMETRY
---------------------------------

3.1 Replace the provisional chassis body with measured geometry.

    Current simulator assumption:
    - Box half-extents: 145 x 85 x 30 mm.
    - Lumped chassis mass: 2 kg.

    Measure/model:
    - Full external footprint, including bumpers and protruding hardware.
    - Deck and underside clearance.
    - Caster/support-wheel geometry if present.
    - Battery, Jetson, motor-board, and sensor masses.
    - Total mass.
    - Centre of mass.
    - Inertia tensor or a reasonable distributed-mass approximation.

3.2 Verify wheel locations and dimensions on the assembled robot.

    Current simulator values:
    - Track: 216 mm.
    - Wheelbase: 248 mm.
    - Tire diameter: 67.50 mm.
    - Tread width: 26.50 mm.

    Measure loaded rolling radius, not only unloaded tire diameter. Check whether tire
    deformation changes the effective radius under the robot's weight.

3.3 Use the robot's true driving-forward end as the simulator +x direction.

    The physical chassis labels are misleading: the end labelled "front" is not the
    direction confirmed as forward by the motor wiring. Camera pose, LiDAR zero,
    collision angle, wheel side, and simulated +x must all refer to the same physical
    direction.

3.4 Match actual contact sensing.

    The simulator currently provides a perfect collision flag, contact point, and
    contact angle. The current physical robot has no equivalent contact sensor in the
    inspected hardware path.

    For the first MVP, either:
    - Disable simulated tactile/contact input during matching training, or
    - Keep physical policy input permanently at collision=false and angle=0.

    If bump switches are later added, reproduce their placement, activation threshold,
    blind spots, debounce, and left/right/front angular convention in simulation.


4. WHEELS, TIRES, AND SKID-STEER MOTION
---------------------------------------

4.1 Measure and replace the ideal tire-ground parameters.

    Calibrate on the actual demo surface:
    - Longitudinal traction.
    - Lateral scrub/slip during skid steering.
    - Rolling resistance.
    - Static friction and breakaway behavior.
    - Left/right differences.
    - Tire compliance.
    - Behavior on seams, cables, thresholds, and uneven flooring.

    The current MuJoCo friction values are provisional and are not derived from tests.

4.2 Measure the real relationship between left/right wheel commands and chassis
    linear/angular velocity.

    Record straight runs and in-place turns at several positive and negative speeds.
    Fit the effective track width, speed scale, turn scale, and slip behavior. The
    geometric 216 mm track alone will not predict skid-steer yaw accurately.

4.3 Verify per-wheel direction and side mapping against the simulator.

    Confirmed physical board mapping:
    - M1: back right wheel, treated as left drive side.
    - M2: back left wheel, treated as right drive side.
    - M3: front right wheel, treated as left drive side.
    - M4: front left wheel, treated as right drive side.
    - Motor polarity signs: 1, -1, -1, 1.

    The simulator abstracts this into left/right sides, so the physical command adapter
    must preserve the same forward and positive-yaw convention.


5. MOTORS, ENCODERS, AND MOTOR CONTROLLER
-----------------------------------------

5.1 Keep the confirmed motor profile and remove old assumptions from comparisons.

    Physical/current intended profile:
    - Yahboom L-type 520 motors.
    - 12 V.
    - 40:1 reduction.
    - 11 encoder lines.
    - x4 quadrature decoding.
    - 1760 counts per wheel revolution.
    - 67.5 mm configured wheel diameter.

    Some legacy hardware examples still configure 30:1 and 67.0 mm. Do not use those
    values to tune the simulator.

5.2 Replace the ideal MuJoCo velocity actuators with measured motor/controller
    dynamics.

    Measure/model:
    - Command units and actual wheel speed.
    - Closed-loop speed-controller response time.
    - Dead zone and minimum sustainable speed.
    - Acceleration and deceleration limits.
    - Braking behavior at a zero speed command.
    - Forward/reverse asymmetry.
    - Command latency and jitter through Jetson -> USB -> Pico -> I2C -> motor board.
    - Saturation under load.
    - Torque-speed curve.
    - Motor/wheel inertia and gearbox backlash.

    Current simulator actuator assumptions:
    - Ideal velocity servo with provisional gain/damping/inertia.
    - Maximum wheel speed 31.416 rad/s.
    - Rated continuous torque limit 0.431493 N m.

5.3 Model electrical effects that change motion during a demo.

    Measure or approximate:
    - Battery voltage at rest and under load.
    - Voltage sag during starts and turns.
    - Differences as the battery discharges.
    - Current limiting and thermal derating.

5.4 Validate encoder behavior if it is used by the goal/localization system.

    Include:
    - Correct sign per wheel.
    - Quantization at 1760 counts/revolution.
    - Sampling/update rate.
    - Counter reset/wrap behavior.
    - Missed counts and wheel slip.

    Encoders do not measure slip-corrected chassis motion.


6. PHYSICAL UPDATE RATES AND LATENCIES TO REPRODUCE
---------------------------------------------------

The simulator should ultimately reproduce the complete hardware timing chain:

    - Policy/RNN target tick: 50 Hz / 20 ms.
    - Cameras: 30 Hz, with repeated frames between updates.
    - LiDAR: measured 6-12 Hz, with one rolling revolution per update.
    - Goal manager: its measured update rate, latency, and dropout behavior.
    - Motor command transport: measured latency and jitter.
    - Encoder/odometry: measured update rate and delay, if used.

Sensor values should be sample-and-held and timestamped independently. Do not update
all simulated sensors synchronously at the 50 Hz policy rate when the real devices do
not behave that way.


7. PHYSICAL DEMO ENVIRONMENT
----------------------------

For the quickest useful sim-to-real improvement, make one simulator scene match the
actual hackathon demo area:

    - Floor material and traction.
    - Lighting level and direction.
    - Arena/wall dimensions.
    - Sizes, heights, shapes, colours, and materials of demo obstacles.
    - Objects that the 210 mm LiDAR plane will miss.
    - Whether a visible physical goal marker exists.

The current simulator primarily uses fixed cylindrical obstacles and a visible white
goal sphere. If the real goal is managed only by a separate system, remove or hide the
simulated goal sphere in the matching scene so the camera policy does not depend on a
visual object that does not exist on hardware.


MVP PRIORITY ORDER
------------------

Before interpreting live shadow-loop behavior:

1. Confirm the robot's true forward direction across cameras, LiDAR, and wheels.
2. Calibrate or at least visually verify both camera poses and FOVs.
3. Recheck LiDAR scale, zero angle, height, tilt, and actual scan frequency.
4. Reproduce 30 Hz camera sample-and-hold and the measured LiDAR rate in simulation.
5. Decide that physical collision input is zero for the MVP and test the model under
   that same condition in simulation.
6. Hide the simulated visual goal if the physical demo has no matching goal marker.

After the first shadow loop works:

7. Measure drivetrain speed/yaw response and effective skid-steer track width.
8. Measure mass, centre of mass, tire friction/slip, dead zone, and motor latency.
9. Add realistic camera/LiDAR noise, dropouts, and rolling acquisition.
10. Update training or fine-tune using the measured hardware model.
